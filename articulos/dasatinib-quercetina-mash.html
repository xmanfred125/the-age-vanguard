Human trial · Phase 2 · 31 people

Clearing “zombie” cells improved liver fibrosis in almost half of those treated

A randomized phase 2 trial (Amsterdam, published in Nature Metabolism in 2026) tested dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) in 31 people with fibrotic MASH. After three 7-week cycles, 47% of those treated improved at least one fibrosis stage, versus 7% on placebo; MASH resolution was 53% versus 7%. Tissue analysis confirmed fewer senescence signatures. It is proof of principle with 31 people: larger phase 3 trials are needed before recommending it. And a warning about the number: the effect’s confidence interval crosses 1, so the result is promising but not confirmatory.

Verified against the primary source

Koning et al., “Senolytics dasatinib and quercetin in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis: a proof-of-principle randomized, controlled trial”, Nature Metabolism, 1 Oct 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01643-4. PMID: 42823536. TRUTH trial, NCT05506488, Amsterdam UMC, single centre. Phase 2, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled: 31 participants (median 56 years, 76% men, 58% with type 2 diabetes), 17 on D+Q and 14 on placebo; dasatinib 100 mg/day + quercetin 1,000 mg/day, 3 days a week for 3 weeks, repeated in three 7-week cycles (21 weeks).

Caution to add

The authors themselves call it “proof of principle” and “hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory”. The relative risk is 6.59 with a confidence interval of 0.93–46.53 (it crosses 1, meaning it technically includes the possibility of no effect), and in the most conservative analysis of the 4 missing biopsies the significance disappears. No changes in non-invasive tests (FIB-4, liver stiffness). It does not justify self-medicating with dasatinib: it is a leukaemia drug, not approved for MASH or for anti-aging.
