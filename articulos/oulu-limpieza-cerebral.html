Oulu: a nitroglycerin pill and a vibrating mattress help the brain clean itself in awake people

Published in Nature Communications, November 3, 2026.

Researchers at the University of Oulu (Finland) tested whether the glymphatic system — the network that clears waste from the brain and works mostly during sleep — can be sped up in awake people. In 29 healthy adults (27 to 61 years old, mean 44), one session combined sublingual nitroglycerin, which dilates blood vessels, with about 40 minutes of whole-body vibration (22–50 Hz, to activate Piezo1 channels). After the session, blood beta-amyloid 42 rose by about 35% (from 2.6 to 3.5 pg/ml), beta-amyloid 40 from 38.2 to 50.5 pg/ml and GFAP from 65.2 to 85.1 pg/ml, consistent with more protein leaving the brain.

What happened

Randomized crossover design: each volunteer received vibration alone and nitroglycerin + vibration on different days; there was no placebo pill.

Why it matters

It is the first demonstration in awake humans that brain drainage can be modulated with tools that already exist — a possible path to preventing neurodegenerative disease.

What comes next

A trial in 60 people with mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer’s, planned for next year.

Caution

A single session, 29 healthy middle-aged people, no brain amyloid measured by PET and no cognition measured. The rise in blood may also reflect slower peripheral breakdown. The combination is dangerous with PDE5 inhibitors or in people with orthostatic hypotension; it must not be tried outside a study. Sleep remains the best-supported way to protect brain clearance.
