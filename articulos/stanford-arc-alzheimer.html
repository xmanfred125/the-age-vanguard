Stanford: a brain-activity pattern predicts Alzheimer’s years before memory fails

Published in Science Advances, November 4, 2026.

Researchers at Stanford and colleagues analyzed resting brain activity in adults aged 75 and older at the two extremes of the spectrum: people with Alzheimer’s and “superagers”, with the memory of people decades younger. From those patterns they created the ARC score (Alzheimer’s resilience-vulnerability connectome), which distinguished superagers from people with Alzheimer’s with about 85% accuracy and predicted cognitive decline better than demographics, amyloid and tau biomarkers, and genetics. The more Alzheimer-like the pattern, the more likely decline, plaque onset and diagnosis were in the following 12 years.

What happened

The ARC was derived from resting functional MRI and validated against the participants’ follow-up.

Why it matters

An early and potentially modifiable biomarker would allow intervention before symptoms appear, when treatments have more room to work.

What comes next

For now the ARC is a tracking tool, not a diagnosis; the authors stress that a score is not a destiny.

Caution

Observational study; predicting is not preventing.
