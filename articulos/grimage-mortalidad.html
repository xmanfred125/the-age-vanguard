Epigenetic clocks predict mortality: GrimAge raises risk by 46% per standard deviation

Preprint on medRxiv, October 21, 2026.

A systematic review and meta-analysis of 125 studies with 380,705 participants, led by Andrea Maier’s group (National University of Singapore), evaluated 48 DNA-methylation clocks. Each one-standard-deviation increase in GrimAge acceleration was associated with a 46% higher risk of death from any cause in general populations (HR 1.46; 95% CI 1.38–1.55), or 9% per year of acceleration.

What happened

Of 42,023 records reviewed, 125 papers were included and 53 in the hazard-ratio meta-analyses.

Why it matters

It is the largest synthesis yet of epigenetic clocks’ predictive power, and it strengthens their use as a measure of biological aging in trials.

What comes next

The supplementary effect data, which were not published with the preprint, are still needed.

Caution

Preprint without peer review; the included studies are observational, so the association does not prove that epigenetic age causes death, and clock acceleration remains a surrogate.
