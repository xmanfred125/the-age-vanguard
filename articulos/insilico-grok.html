Software launch · No clinical validation

Insilico releases for free a “longevity superintelligence” that predicts how your biomarkers will change

On November 6, Insilico Medicine open-sourced Longevity Grok (PLSI), a multi-agent system for the Grok Bot platform, developed with David Sinclair (Harvard). It unifies medical records, wearables and epigenetic analyses, proposes interventions and then scores whether the biomarker moved as predicted. It is free (MIT license) but requires a Grok Bot account.

Caution, in the text itself

It is research and education software; it does not diagnose or prescribe, its outputs are not reviewed by a doctor, and its predictions have no published clinical validation. As the field itself acknowledges: no tool has been shown to add a year of life to an already healthy person who sleeps, exercises and gets checkups.
