At 81, a 50 km world record with the VO2max of a 20-year-old

Published in Frontiers in Physiology, January 12, 2026.

An 81-year-old runner set a new 50 km world record in the over-80 category in 2025 (4 h 47 min 39 s) at the Masters Championships in Málaga (Spain). Two weeks after the record, his measured VO2max was 52.8 ml/kg/min, the highest recorded in octogenarians according to the authors, equivalent to the 70th percentile of healthy men aged 20 to 30, with a large fat-oxidation capacity. He is also the current world marathon champion in the over-80 category (3 h 39 min 10 s in Bucharest, 2024).

What happened

A full physiological case study after the record, with exercise testing and substrate analysis.

Why it matters

It is the cleanest proof that the physiological ceiling of aging is much higher than assumed, and that lifelong training preserves aerobic capacity.

What comes next

It is a single exceptional case, not a recipe; it cannot be generalized to the whole older population.

Caution

Case study (n = 1); the athlete’s name was not verified in the sources consulted, so it is not published here.
