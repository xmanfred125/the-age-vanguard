Human trial · Phase 1

For the first time, a cell-rejuvenation therapy reaches people — and the first three suffered no serious harm

Life Biosciences reported on October 8 the first human data for ER-100: three glaucoma patients received the lowest dose; in two, the visual field improved at 56 days. It is an early signal, not proof.

What happened

ER-100 uses three Yamanaka factors (OSK: OCT4, SOX2 and KLF4) — without c-Myc, the factor linked to cancer risk — to restore part of the cell’s epigenetic configuration without turning it into a stem cell. It is given by injection into the eye and activated with eight weeks of daily oral doxycycline. The FDA authorized the trial in January 2026; the first patient was treated on June 9. The trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07290244) plans up to 18 participants aged 40 to 85, in Boston, New York, Los Angeles and Charleston, with follow-up of up to five years.

Why it matters

It is the first time partial reprogramming — demonstrated in mice in David Sinclair’s Harvard lab (Nature, 2020) — is tested in people. Vision is independence: glaucoma damages the optic nerve irreversibly.

What comes next

The independent safety board recommended tripling the dose for the next group; that phase is under way. The higher-dose results are what to watch.

What is not known / Caution

Three people, no placebo group, open-label trial, company-announced data, not yet peer-reviewed. The visual-field improvement may partly reflect a learning effect on the test. The trial is designed to test safety, not efficacy. It does not prove human aging has been reversed.

None of these therapies can be bought or received outside a trial.
