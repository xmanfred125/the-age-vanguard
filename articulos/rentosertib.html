Rentosertib: the first AI-designed drug enters phase 3 and moves aging clocks

First patient dosed in GENESIS-IPF-3, September 10, 2026; clock analysis in Nature Biotechnology, September 7, 2026.

Insilico Medicine dosed the first patient with rentosertib in GENESIS-IPF-3, a phase 3 trial in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis with 320 planned participants at 47 centres in China, over 52 weeks (NCT07687459). It is, according to the company, the first phase 3 trial of a generative-AI-designed drug. In the earlier phase 2a analysis (42 patients, 12 weeks), the drug moved six proteomic aging clocks toward younger profiles: the four chronological clocks fell by 2.71 to 3.46 years in the 60 mg arm at 4 weeks, with no significant change in the two clocks trained for mortality.

What happened

The drug, AI-designed against the TNIK target, moved from phase 2a to phase 3; the clock analysis was done on blood already collected in phase 2a.

Why it matters

It is the strongest signal yet of an AI-discovered drug with a measurable effect on aging clocks in people, and the first time those clocks have been built into a clinical trial.

What comes next

Phase 3 measures lung function (FVC), not biological age; it will take 3–4 years to a possible approval, and the authors themselves acknowledge that in patients with fibrosis the anti-aging effect cannot be separated from the effect on the diseased lung.

Caution

Clocks are surrogate markers, not proof of slower aging; the trial treats fibrosis, not aging.
