The largest genetic study of Alzheimer’s: 48 new risk loci in 2.8 million people

Published in Nature, October 28, 2026.

A genome-wide association study with 109,479 Alzheimer’s cases, 74,141 proxy cases and more than 2.6 million controls identified 127 independent risk loci, 48 of them new. The work estimates heritability from common variants at 16%, almost triple what earlier studies explained, and points to dysregulation of microglia and vulnerable neurons as the core of the disease.

What happened

The largest Alzheimer’s GWAS to date, with multi-ancestry analysis.

Why it matters

It triples the explained share of heritability and sharpens polygenic prediction, as well as pointing to concrete biological targets (microglia).

What comes next

Most of the new loci are not functionally confirmed, and gene assignment is predictive.

Caution

About 91% of the effective sample was of European ancestry, so prediction is weaker in other populations; proxy cases rely on family reports, not diagnosis.
