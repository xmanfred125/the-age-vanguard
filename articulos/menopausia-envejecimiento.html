Earlier menopause, faster biological aging

Published in Mechanisms of Ageing and Development, October 13, 2026.

A meta-analysis of 29 studies with 550,550 women, led from the National University of Singapore, found that age at menopause and reproductive history are associated with the pace of biological aging measured by DNA-methylation clocks. Earlier menopause showed one of the clearest associations: each year earlier at menopause was associated with about 0.08 years (around one month) more epigenetic age acceleration by GrimAge, in an analysis of 5,920 women.

What happened

Systematic review and meta-analysis of the literature on reproductive milestones and DNAm clocks.

Why it matters

It opens a path to identifying women at higher risk earlier, and to studying whether reproductive health is a window into aging overall.

What comes next

The researchers themselves warn that the evidence is preliminary and that it is unclear whether these measures improve current clinical tools.

Caution

These are associations, not cause and effect.
