NAD+: the supplement raises blood NAD+, but does not improve function

NMN in npj Aging, September 25, 2026; nicotinamide riboside in Alzheimer’s & Dementia, July 21, 2026.

Two placebo-controlled randomized trials cool the enthusiasm for NAD+ boosters. In npj Aging, adults aged 65 and older took 1,250 mg of NMN daily or placebo for 24 weeks: blood NAD+ and its metabolites rose clearly, but there was no significant improvement in the primary outcome (hearing) or in measures of physical performance. At the University of Delaware, 12 weeks of nicotinamide riboside (NR) in older adults with mild cognitive impairment raised blood NAD+ without improving cognition, cardiovascular function or cerebral blood flow. And in the NOPARK trial (JAMA, October 2026), 500 mg of NR twice a day for 52 weeks in 410 people with early Parkinson’s gave no benefit; the placebo group even scored slightly better on the primary symptom score.

What happened

Three independent trials, same pattern: the biochemistry moves, function does not.

Why it matters

It is the week’s scientific-honesty story for a supplements market worth billions: raising a marker is not rejuvenation.

What comes next

Larger, longer trials are needed to confirm whether there is any real benefit.

Caution

The NMN trial was funded by MIRAILAB BIOSCIENCE, which supplied the NMN. The NOPARK details come from a secondary summary (SECONDARY SOURCE) and should be confirmed against the JAMA article before publishing exact figures.
